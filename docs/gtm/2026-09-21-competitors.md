# Linty: competitor and distribution research

Research date: 21 September 2026. This covers ten relevant products or substitutes, rather than claiming exhaustive coverage of every dictation app. Product facts come primarily from vendor pages and repositories. “GTM interpretation” describes the strategy suggested by observable activity; private acquisition spend, conversion rates, retention, and channel profitability were not available. Prices are the public offers observed on this date and may change; annual equivalents are labeled.

**The observable patterns suggest four routes into the market.** Paid dictation vendors use product demonstrations, trials, creator relationships, and repeat billing to support acquisition. Open-source projects make the runnable product, repository, and contribution path easy to reach. Broader transcription products attract people through specific jobs such as meetings or subtitle exports. Every can introduce Monologue to an existing audience and bundle it with other products. These are descriptions of visible mechanisms, not proof of how much growth each mechanism caused.

For Linty, the relevant combination is a free, easy-to-try app plus convincing workflow footage, founder participation, and useful public engineering work. Buying attention would be premature before knowing whether users return. Replicating a paid affiliate scheme makes little sense while Linty has no purchase to fund commissions.

**Linty's strongest present proposition is a particular combination of choices.** It provides free signed/notarized downloads, local recognition and optional local English cleanup, language-based model preparation, local history, a dictionary, and MIT-licensed app source for Apple Silicon Macs. Those choices support a useful promise of accessible local dictation. They do not establish market exclusivity, superior accuracy, or superior onboarding. [Linty documentation](https://github.com/shekhardtu/linty), [model notices](../../src-tauri/licenses/MODELS.md).

Against a cloud-dependent workflow, demonstrate offline dictation after setup. Against a paid packaged app, make the free download and absence of account setup clear. Against Handy, FluidVoice, and OpenWhispr, acknowledge the overlap and invite a workflow comparison. Against built-in dictation, demonstrate history, the dictionary, and English cleanup in a task the prospective user actually performs. If those benefits do not matter to a particular person, they are not the first target customer.

**A credible comparison needs measurements beyond inference speed.** Linty's published “30-minute file in 4.97 seconds” is a warm Parakeet inference result on one M3 Pro using synthetic English. It excludes setup, model loading, recording, UI, cleanup, and paste. It is interesting technical evidence, but cannot support “fastest dictation app,” “instant first run,” or a claim about real-world accent accuracy. [Methodology](../TRANSCRIPTION-CAPACITY.md).

Before publishing performance comparisons, use a repeatable set of real, consented or explicitly synthetic tasks, state which, and test the same tasks across the selected products. Include current versions/settings, microphone, Mac hardware, cold and warm starts, release-to-paste time, failed insertions, and correction time. Report multiple runs, medians and variability. Compare cleaned text on preserved meaning and usable output, because word error alone can penalize deliberate formatting. Network conditions matter for cloud products. Do not announce a win until the experiment is complete.

**Some search results require particular care.** Several comparison articles were competitor-owned or affiliate-supported, so they were used only for discovery, not as the basis for product facts. A FluidVoice-branded domain claimed official status, but the project metadata linked to altic.dev/fluid; this report uses the repository-linked destination. MacWhisper has dictation today even though some third-party summaries describe it as file-only. Old Monologue launch pricing differs from its current page. Verify prices and features again when publishing a comparison.

The practical research conclusion is to compete through a specific, proven user experience and distribution discipline. No evidence here justifies building a second app to create a supposed feature gap. The next research investment should be conversations and observed use by the first 20 target users.
